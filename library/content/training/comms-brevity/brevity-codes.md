# Brevity Codes

Brevity codes are **single words with agreed-upon meanings.**

Instead of explaining an entire situation every time, one word communicates the same idea instantly.

Use them when everyone understands them.

If using plain English would be clearer, use plain English.

---

# High Priority

These are the brevity words every player should know.

## Copy

"I received your transmission."

It **does not** mean you understand or will comply.

Example:

```
Bravo, hold this building.

Copy.
```

---

## Wilco

"I understand and will comply."

("Will Comply.")

Example:

```
Move to the church.

Wilco.
```

Unlike **Copy**, this confirms you intend to carry out the order.

---

## Say Again

"Repeat your last transmission."

Use this instead of:

```
What?
```

Example:

```
Say again your last.
```

---

## Break

Separates two unrelated pieces of information.

Instead of making two transmissions, you can combine them.

Example:

```
Enemy squad north of church.

Break.

We are moving east.
```

---

## Stand By

"Wait."

The conversation is not over.

Example:

```
Stand by.
```

Useful when checking a map or coordinating with someone else.

---

## Wait, Out

Sometimes you need a longer pause.

```
Wait, out.
```

The conversation has ended for now.

The other station should expect another call later.

---

## Roger

Historically, this meant "received."

Today, many players simply use **Copy** instead.

Either is acceptable if your unit is consistent.

---

## Negative

No.

Instead of:

```
Nope.
```

Use:

```
Negative.
```

---

## Affirm

Yes.

Shorter and clearer than "yeah."

---

## Out

The conversation is finished.

Do not expect another reply.

Example:

```
Objective secured.

Out.
```

---

## Radio Check

Checks whether your radio works.

Example:

```
Bravo, this is Alpha.

Radio check.
```

Response:

```
Loud and clear.
```

or

```
Weak but readable.
```

Only perform radio checks when necessary.

---

# Contact Brevity

These words become useful once your unit grows.

## Contact

Enemy has been located.

```
Contact!

Enemy squad west.
```

---

## Troops in Contact (TIC)

Friendly forces are actively engaged.

Usually implies immediate support may be required.

---

## Winchester

Out of ammunition.

Originally referred to expending all weapons.

In infantry games, many units simply use it to mean:

```
I'm completely out.
```

---

## Bingo

Minimum fuel to safely return.

Useful only if your unit uses an aircraft.

---

## RTB

Return to Base.

Usually spoken as the letters:

```
R-T-B
```

---

## Oscar Mike

"On the move."

Common enough to recognize, but plain English is equally acceptable.

---

## Rally

Return to the designated rally point.

Example:

```
Rally at checkpoint Bravo.
```

---

# Aviation

These mostly matter for helicopter crews and CAS.

## LZ

Landing Zone.

```
LZ secure.
```

---

## Dustoff

Medical evacuation. <br/>
Can also mean a craft is gaining altitude and breaking away from a site.

---

## Hot LZ

Landing zone is receiving enemy fire.

---

## Cold LZ

Landing zone appears safe.

---

## Wave Off

Abort the landing. / Do not continue the approach. <br/>
Can also be used to mean breaking contact from the AO.

---

# Use Sparingly

Using brevity correctly makes communication faster.

Using brevity nobody understands makes communication slower.

If you're unsure whether everyone knows a term, just say it in plain English.
